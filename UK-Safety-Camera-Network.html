Our review and scope #

UK Safety Camera Network Ltd (USCN) is a UK security company specialising in IP CCTV installation, maintenance and tailored surveillance system design. It also plans to develop a community safety camera network supported by its commercial activities.

Our assessment covers the site consultation, design process and completed four-camera installation. We found the finished work very good and the technical planning thoughtful, while identifying some shortcomings in presentation, telephone access and time taken on site.

What we reviewed: The initial site assessment, planning, customer communication, attendance and presentation on installation day, installation duration and the completed four-camera CCTV installation. Our observations include both strengths and areas for improvement.

Experts in IP CCTV design and installation planning #

Based on our first-hand interactions, we consider USCN highly knowledgeable in IP CCTV technology, networked recording, camera positioning and system design. Founder Bert Scanlon demonstrated years of practical experience and a detailed understanding of how surveillance systems should be designed around specific security objectives.

During our assessment, he explained why a system intended to detect break-ins should focus on approaches, entrances, exits and vulnerable openings, while a theft-focused system may require more targeted internal coverage of valuable assets or transaction areas.

A camera should do one job well rather than several jobs poorly.

That purpose-led design philosophy stood out to us. We did not feel we were being pushed toward a standard camera package or unnecessary equipment.

Detailed surveys, clear explanations and clean-installation planning #

Our initial site visit was thorough. Bert reviewed sight lines, camera angles, power, network connectivity, cable routes and the practical constraints of our premises. He used on-site photographs to help demonstrate proposed coverage and revisited some early recommendations when different camera types or positions appeared more effective.

USCN then produced a detailed written proposal covering:

  • Recommended cameras, their positions and their individual purposes
  • Coverage goals, sight lines and likely security benefits
  • Power requirements and network architecture
  • Cabling and drilling routes, with attention to cable security
  • How to minimise mess and disruption to day-to-day business operations

We were particularly impressed by the willingness to work within demanding requirements rather than overlook them for convenience. The planning showed real consideration for a tidy, practical installation and careful cable management.

Responsive, flexible and customer-focused #

USCN handled our initial inquiry quickly and professionally, making sure we understood the proposed service before commercial discussions advanced. We were invited to review and negotiate contractual terms rather than simply accept a fixed package.

We agreed to a somewhat higher installation price in exchange for enhanced aftercare suited to our needs, without committing to a conventional monthly maintenance plan. USCN also advertises ongoing maintenance services for customers who prefer continued technical assistance.

We valued the company's willingness to explain the options without pressuring us to purchase support we did not need.

A fascinating model connecting private and public safety #

USCN operates in commercial security while developing a community-focused public safety initiative. Its stated ambition is to use revenue from private CCTV work to help finance a wider network of hosted public safety cameras.

We see this as an innovative, almost Robin Hood-style approach: commercial security work helps support infrastructure intended to benefit the wider community. We consider the model potentially pioneering, without claiming it is the first of its kind.

Importantly, USCN's published support information has described the wider community camera network as pre-launch. Its planned network should not be confused with a fully deployed public safety system.

From the founder: a public safety mission built into the business #

In comments provided for this article, founder Bert Scanlon describes UK Safety Camera Network Ltd as a business created with a public safety mission at its core. His stated aim is to use high-quality commercial CCTV work to help finance wider community safety initiatives, while giving customers clear information about the systems being recommended to them.

The founder’s approach, in summary: Customers should understand what is being recommended, why it is appropriate for their property and what alternatives they have. USCN says its proposals are intended to be sufficiently detailed for customers to review the reasoning and request changes before work proceeds.

Scanlon acknowledges that USCN may not be the cheapest CCTV installer. Instead, he emphasises bespoke security assessments, justified equipment choices and long-term value. The company says it aims to recommend durable hardware suited to each project rather than simply the options that generate the highest immediate margin.

He also highlights the importance of people's ability to request access to personal information held about them, alongside responsible data handling in surveillance systems. Such access remains subject to applicable data protection law and any relevant exemptions.

How the company says its profits support public safety

USCN is structured as a company limited by guarantee, rather than a conventional share-capital company. Scanlon says the intention is to direct surplus resources toward developing the organisation and furthering its public safety mission, rather than paying shareholder dividends. This describes the founder's stated business philosophy; it is not an independent audit of how funds have been used.

For prospective customers, the central message is that transparent design decisions, quality workmanship and long-term reliability are intended to benefit both the paying customer and the wider community. We found this philosophy consistent with the detailed planning and explanations provided during our own consultation.

Camera hosting: an opportunity to see the approach first #

One aspect we especially appreciated was USCN's suggestion that we consider hosting one of its public safety cameras before committing to a larger commercial project. Under the arrangement presented to us, USCN would provide and maintain the camera without charging for the camera equipment or installation.

The hosting arrangement included an initial 90-day period and a 30-day notice provision for removal. Exact eligibility, ongoing obligations and removal conditions should always be checked against the current written hosting agreement; a free installation does not necessarily mean there are no responsibilities for the host.

We liked the opportunity to understand the company's methods and working relationship before investing in a larger paid installation.

Areas for improvement: presentation, callbacks and installation time #

Arrival and on-site presentation

During the initial site assessment, the team’s approach was appropriate and professional. For the installation itself, however, the two installers arrived by bicycle with equipment carried on a trailer, while a hire company delivered the ladder they needed. The reviewer felt that arriving in a suitably equipped company van would have presented a more polished, conventional professional image. This is a comment about presentation and logistics, not evidence of poor workmanship or an unsafe installation. The work was completed to the required standard.

Telephone callback arrangements

Calls to the advertised telephone line were handled through a callback system rather than being answered directly. In our experience, this could be frustrating: a missed return call, particularly when a phone was on silent, meant calling again and re-entering the callback process. The result occasionally felt like a game of telephone tag. On the positive side, callbacks were generally prompt and questions were answered efficiently once contact was made. A direct-answer option, clearer callback timing or an alternative contact route could improve accessibility.

Time taken for a four-camera installation

The two-person team took longer than the reviewer expected to install four cameras. Some of that time was spent on useful technical decisions, including checking camera positions, potential sunlight interference and suitable cable routes. That care contributed to the quality of the final installation, but the reviewer felt the job could at times have progressed more efficiently. No precise installation duration or benchmark was recorded, so this is a subjective observation rather than a measured productivity comparison.

Overall balance

These points concern the polish and convenience of the service, rather than the quality of the completed CCTV system. The installers remained professional throughout, the installation met the agreed standard and the finished result was very good. The criticisms are nevertheless relevant to the full customer experience.

Our verdict: four-star CCTV installation and service #

★★★★☆ — 4/5 SSR editorial rating. The completed CCTV installation was very good, and the team demonstrated strong technical judgment in camera positioning and cabling. We also recorded reservations about installation-day presentation, the callback-only telephone arrangement and the length of time taken for four cameras. Our overall experience was positive, particularly regarding the finished installation and willingness to consider technical details carefully. Customers who value fast direct telephone access, a conventional company-van arrival or a shorter installation appointment should discuss those expectations with USCN in advance.
